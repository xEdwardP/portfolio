## The problem

Coordinating peer tutoring across an institution with multiple campuses takes more than
connecting students with tutors. Each campus needs to manage its students, approve who can
teach and respond to support requests, while the Director maintains a shared academic
catalog and oversees operations.

Macaw brings those workflows together in a platform that distinguishes **paid tutoring**
from **scholarship tutoring**. Paid sessions require financial tracking; scholarship
sessions record hours without charging students.

## Architecture decisions

### Permissions by role and campus

The application has five roles: student, paid tutor, scholarship tutor, campus coordinator
and Director. The Director manages campuses, coordinators and the global catalog of
faculties and subjects. Each coordinator manages their campus's students, invitations,
subsidies, tutor approvals and support requests.

Registration requires an institutional email domain, an active campus and email
verification. Tutor approval is a separate step: teaching at another campus requires a
verified membership there. Subjects and bookings are validated against the student's campus.
Revoking an approval prevents new bookings while preserving existing session records.

### Separate accounting for money and scholarship hours

Booking a paid session reserves funds in escrow. Student confirmation, cancellations and
disputes settle or refund funds through an **auditable ledger**. Manual fund and subsidy
operations require an idempotency key to prevent duplicates.

Tutors can only mark sessions as delivered after they end. A worker processes automatic
confirmation and pending events. Scholarship sessions award hours to the corresponding
program without charges or withdrawals.

The active currency is USD and **PayPal operates exclusively in sandbox**. Live payments
remain blocked, including in production installations.

### Shared scheduling and agreed rescheduling

Tutors configure weekly availability, date-specific absences and their **Google
Calendar/Meet** connection. Absences block new time slots across all their campuses without
changing the weekly schedule or automatically cancelling existing bookings.

Students and tutors can propose a new time; only the other party can accept it. The original
time remains in effect until acceptance, when availability, absences, scheduling conflicts,
campus approval, subject and session format are checked again. Duration, price, currency,
commission and scholarship remain unchanged, without moving funds or awarding hours.

The worker updates the original Google Calendar event with retries, preserves the Meet link
and discards reminders for previous times. The interface shows when synchronization is
still pending.

### Recommendations from eligible candidates

AI recommendations start with active, verified tutors who teach subjects at the student's
campus and have bookable availability within the next 14 days. The provider receives
subjects, ratings, rates, days and anonymous candidate codes, without names, emails or
account identifiers. When the provider is not configured, recommendations use a fallback
with valid tutors; review summaries require the provider.

When a search returns no results, students can request help. Their campus coordinator
responds and may suggest an eligible tutor. The suggestion does not reserve time or move
funds; the student decides whether to book. Only the requester and their campus coordinator
can view the request history.

### Separate API and worker processes

The frontend uses React 19, Vite, React Router, TanStack Query, Zustand and Tailwind CSS.
The backend combines Node.js, Express, Prisma and PostgreSQL 18, with feature modules and
shared components for security, auditing, events and accounting.

Browser sessions use HttpOnly cookies, CSRF checks and permissions scoped by role and
campus. Notifications persist in PostgreSQL and update through Socket.io. The worker
coordinates tasks with PostgreSQL locks and processes account emails, reminders, automatic
confirmations and pending events.

## Outcome

A tutoring platform for a single institution with multiple campuses, with Spanish and
English interfaces and error messages, plus light and dark themes. Each role has its own
workflows, from finding tutors and booking sessions to managing scholarships, subsidies,
disputes and withdrawals.

The Director has an operations dashboard for reviewing the worker, event queue, reminders
and reconciliation. Integrations require configuration and validation in each installation;
Google Calendar needs valid tutor authorization and an active worker to synchronize changes.
