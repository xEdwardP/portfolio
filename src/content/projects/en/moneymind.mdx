## The problem

A personal finance app is, underneath, a CRUD with charts. The risk is not complexity: it is
spending weeks building create-edit-delete screens that have been solved a thousand times,
and arriving late to the only thing that matters: the numbers adding up.

There is also a specific trap: a budget's accumulated spend. It is easy to compute it by
summing transactions every time someone opens the screen. It is easy, and it is why the panel
gets slow once history grows.

## Architecture decisions

### Filament for everything already solved

The entire application is delivered through a **Filament 3.3** panel. Authentication, CRUD,
filters, notifications and charts come out of the box.

This is not laziness. It is a choice about where effort goes. Hand-writing category forms
does not differentiate the product. Budgets working correctly does, and that is where the
custom code lives.

### Spend accumulates on write, not on read

When a transaction of type `gasto` is created, a **model observer** looks for a budget
matching the same user and category within the current month, and increments its
`spentAmount` field.

The budget stores its own total. Reading it is reading a column, not aggregating a table.
Query cost does not grow with account age, and the dashboard chart does not degrade with use.

The honest counterweight: the total is derived data that now lives duplicated, so it depends
on the observer always firing. That is the classic trade-off, taken deliberately.

### Typed categories

Every category is declared as `ingreso` or `gasto`. It is not a decorative label: it is what
lets the observer know which movements affect a budget, and what keeps panel filters and
colour coding coherent without extra logic on each screen.

## Outcome

Movement entries with amount, category, rich-text description, receipt photo and date.
Monthly budget per user and category, with accumulated spend tracking itself. A dashboard
with stat cards and monthly line charts for income and expenses.

The application is pinned to Spanish in `config/app.php`: the product speaks one language
even though its documentation is bilingual.
